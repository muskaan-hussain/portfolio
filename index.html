I — The First Studio

I grew up inside art

A house of paintings, handmade furniture, and objects with history. My father worked at his canvas; I watched, and learned to see.

“You would make a fine artist, my daughter.”— my father, at his easel
II — Architecture & BIM

Then a teacher walked in

She saw the drawings, and my passion for art moved into architecture. The artist in me didn't leave — it changed scale: five years of school, a registered architect, the Celestial Trio in the frame beside you. Then the drawings became models, and the models met the site — cast-stone facades, JFK Terminals 1 & 6, Barclays Center.

III — Giving Back

Then I walked in as the lecturer

After my bachelor's, I returned to Vaishnavi School of Architecture, Hyderabad — this time at the front of the room. With the final-year students I talked about their "Design DNA" in portfolios and CVs, real talk about architecture salaries, building a "Digital Sandbox" online, "AI is Coming for Your Job (Maybe)", and the architecture grind itself. The Principal's letter of appreciation hangs with my father's paintings.

IV — Interiors

This part, I taught myself

Rooms with identity — culture, memory, and routine at human scale. My mentors walked their own ways; I kept walking, alone now, and built Archpainted: 40+ projects designed, 10+ carried through construction as their manager — a Nestlé laboratory, an army building in Delhi, homes and cafés. That is where I learned that drawings and reality part ways on the jobsite.
