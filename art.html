I — Where it all began

Before architecture,
there was a canvas.

I grew up inside art. Our home was a studio disguised as a house — paintings on every wall, handmade furniture, objects that carried history. My father worked at his easel for hours, and I sat beside him, watching pigment turn into light. I didn't know it then, but I was learning to see.

“You would make a fine artist, my daughter.”— my father, at his easel

He taught me that a painting is not decoration — it is a decision about what matters. Composition, patience, restraint. Years later, when a teacher looked at my drawings and told me I saw spaces, not pictures, the artist in me didn't leave. It changed scale. The brush became a pencil, the canvas became a building — and eventually, entire rooms.

These paintings are from that first studio and the years after — the quiet thread that runs through everything I design today.
